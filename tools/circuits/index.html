Key ideas

  • An electric current flows only in a closed circuit: a complete path with no gaps.
  • A battery is the source of electrical energy. A switch opens or closes the circuit.
  • More batteries make the bulbs brighter.
  • More bulbs in series are dimmer. If one is removed, they all go out.
  • Bulbs in parallel are each as bright as a single bulb. If one is removed, the others stay lit.
  • Electrical conductors (such as metals) let electricity pass through. Electrical insulators (such as plastic, wood, rubber and glass) do not.

Think like a scientist

Why do the bulbs get dimmer when more are added in series?

There is only one path, and all the bulbs share the electrical energy from the batteries. With more bulbs, each gets a smaller share.

The lights in your home are connected in parallel. Why is that useful?

Each light has its own path. If one bulb blows, the others stay on, and each light can have its own switch.

Why are electrical wires made of copper and covered in plastic?

Copper is an electrical conductor, so the current flows through it easily. Plastic is an insulator: it stops the current from passing into your hand, which keeps you safe.

You want to find out how the number of batteries affects brightness. What must you keep the same?

Keep the number of bulbs and their arrangement the same, and use the same kind of bulbs. Change only the number of batteries.

Why should you never touch a switch with wet hands?

Tap water conducts electricity. Current could pass through the water and into your body, giving you an electric shock.
