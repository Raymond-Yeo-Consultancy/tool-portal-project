Plant Transport

A plant has tiny tubes inside it. Some carry water and others carry food. Two experiments show where they are and what they do.

Which tree?

Water-carrying tubes or food-carrying tubes?

Key ideas

  • Water-carrying tubes transport water and mineral salts from the roots, up the stem, to all parts of the plant.
  • Food-carrying tubes transport food made in the leaves to all parts of the plant, including the roots.
  • The tubes run through the roots, the stem and the leaves. Together they form the plant's transport system.
  • In a tree trunk, the food-carrying tubes lie just under the bark. The water-carrying tubes are deeper inside.

Think like a scientist

In the celery experiment, why are only some of the tubes red?

The red dye is in the water, so it colours only the tubes that carry water. The food-carrying tubes do not carry water from the beaker, so they are not stained.

Why does the trunk swell above the ring and not below it?

Food travels down from the leaves. It cannot pass the ring, so it collects just above it. Below the ring, no food arrives.

The leaves of the ringed tree stay green at first. Why?

Only the bark and the food-carrying tubes were removed. The water-carrying tubes deeper in the trunk still carry water up to the leaves.

What will happen to the ringed tree after many months?

The roots get no food, so they die. Then the plant cannot take in water, and the whole tree dies.
