Key ideas

  • Heat is a form of energy. Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is. We measure it with a thermometer, in degrees Celsius (°C).
  • Heat flows from a hotter object to a colder one, until both are at the same temperature.
  • An object that gains heat gets warmer. An object that loses heat gets cooler.
  • Good conductors of heat, such as metals, let heat travel through them quickly. Poor conductors, such as wood and plastic, do not.

Think like a scientist

Does “cold” flow from the cold block into the hot block?

No. Only heat flows, and always from the hotter object to the colder one. The hot block cools because it loses heat.

Why do the temperatures change quickly at first and then slowly?

At the start the blocks have very different temperatures, so heat flows quickly. As the temperatures get closer, heat flows more slowly. When they are equal, the flow stops.

Why do cooking pots have metal bases but plastic or wooden handles?

Metal is a good conductor of heat, so heat from the stove reaches the food quickly. Plastic and wood are poor conductors, so the handle stays cool enough to hold.

What makes the spoon race a fair test?

The spoons are the same size, stand in the same hot water, and have the same amount of butter at the same height. Only the material is different.
