Living or non-living?

Key ideas

  • Living things need air, food and water to stay alive. They grow, respond to changes around them, and reproduce.
  • Non-living things do not do all of these things.
  • Living things can be grouped into plants, animals, fungi and bacteria.
  • Animals can be grouped by what they look like and how they live: for example by their body covering, how they move and how they reproduce.

Think like a scientist

A toy robot can move and talk. Why is it not a living thing?

It does not need air, food and water. It does not grow, and it cannot reproduce. A living thing does all of these.

A tree does not move from place to place. Why is it a living thing?

It needs air and water, makes its own food, grows, responds to light and reproduces by making seeds.

Why is a mushroom not a plant?

Plants make their own food. A mushroom cannot: it feeds on dead or living things. It is a fungus and reproduces by spores.

A bat flies and a whale swims. Why are they both mammals?

Both have hair, give birth to their young and feed them with milk. We group animals by features like these, not by where they live.
