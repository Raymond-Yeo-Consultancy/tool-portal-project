Spring Stretch

Hang masses on two springs and measure how much each one stretches. Compare the soft spring with the stiff spring.

Think like a scientist

What pattern do you see?

Every time you add 50 g, Spring A stretches by another 2 cm and Spring B by another 1 cm. The greater the mass, the greater the extension. On the graph, the points form a straight line.

Predict: what would the extension of Spring A be with 350 g?

350 g is 7 lots of 50 g. Spring A stretches 2 cm for each 50 g, so the extension would be 7 × 2 = 14 cm. Its length would be 10 + 14 = 24 cm.

Which spring is stiffer? How can you tell?

Spring B. With the same mass, it has a smaller extension. Its line on the graph is less steep.

To make it a fair test, what must stay the same?

Hang the same mass on both springs, and measure the length in the same way. Change only the spring. Try it: put 150 g on each spring and compare them on the ruler.

Key ideas

  • When a spring is stretched, it exerts a spring force that pulls back towards its original length.
  • The mass is pulled down by gravitational force (its weight). When the mass hangs still, the spring force pulling up balances the gravitational force pulling down.
  • The greater the mass hung on the spring, the greater the extension.
  • When the masses are removed, the spring goes back to its original length (as long as it has not been overstretched).
