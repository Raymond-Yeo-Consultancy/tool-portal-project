How it helps

Key ideas

  • An adaptation is a feature that helps an organism survive in its environment.
  • A structural adaptation is a body part or body covering, such as webbed feet or thick fur.
  • A behavioural adaptation is something the organism does, such as hibernating or rolling into a ball.
  • Adaptations help organisms to move, breathe, get food, protect themselves, and cope with heat, cold or a lack of water.

Think like a scientist

A camel and a cactus both live in the desert. What problem do they share?

There is very little water. The cactus stores water in its stem and loses very little through its spines. The camel can go a long time without food or water.

Is “thick fur” structural or behavioural? What about “hibernating”?

Thick fur is structural: it is part of the animal's body. Hibernating is behavioural: it is something the animal does.

Why would a polar bear struggle to survive in a desert?

Its thick fur and fat keep heat in, so it would overheat. Its white fur would no longer hide it. Adaptations suit the place where an organism lives.

A duck and a fish both live in water. How do they move in different ways?

The duck paddles with its webbed feet at the surface. The fish pushes itself with its tail and steers with its fins, and its streamlined body cuts through the water.
