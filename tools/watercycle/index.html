The water cycle

The water cycle

Key ideas

  • Water changes state when it gains or loses heat.
  • Melting (ice to water) and freezing (water to ice) happen at 0 °C.
  • Boiling (water to steam) happens at 100 °C. Evaporation (water to water vapour) can happen at any temperature.
  • Condensation is water vapour changing into water when it loses heat.
  • While water is changing state, its temperature stays the same.
  • Water evaporates faster when it is warmer, when it is windy, and when the exposed surface area is larger.
  • In the water cycle, water evaporates, condenses into clouds, falls as rain and flows back to the sea.

Think like a scientist

The ice is being heated all the time. Why does its temperature stay at 0 °C while it melts?

The heat it gains is used to change the ice into water, not to raise its temperature. Only when all the ice has melted does the temperature start to rise.

How is evaporation different from boiling?

Both change water into a gas. Boiling happens only at 100 °C, throughout the water, with bubbles. Evaporation happens at any temperature, only at the surface.

Why do wet clothes dry faster when they are spread out on a windy, sunny day?

Spreading them out gives a larger exposed surface area. The Sun makes them warmer and the wind carries the water vapour away. All three make the water evaporate faster.

Water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass. Where do they come from?

From the air. Water vapour in the air touches the cold glass, loses heat and condenses into water droplets.

Why is the water cycle important?

It gives us a continuous supply of fresh water. Rain fills our rivers and reservoirs, and living things need this water to stay alive.
