Key ideas

  • A magnet has two poles: a north pole and a south pole.
  • Like poles repel (push each other away). Unlike poles attract (pull together).
  • Magnets attract magnetic materials such as iron and steel. They do not attract non-magnetic materials such as plastic, wood, glass, copper and aluminium.
  • A magnet is strongest at its poles.
  • We can make a magnet by the stroking method or the electrical method.

Think like a scientist

Are all metals magnetic?

No. Iron and steel are magnetic. Copper, aluminium and gold are metals, but a magnet does not attract them.

How can you tell which magnet is the strongest?

Count how many paper clips each magnet picks up. The magnet that picks up the most is the strongest. Use the same kind of paper clips each time to make the test fair.

How do you know an object is a magnet, and not just a piece of iron?

Bring a magnet near it. Only another magnet can be repelled. A piece of iron is attracted to both poles and is never pushed away.

What happens to the electromagnet when you switch it off?

The nail stops being a magnet and the paper clips fall off. It is a magnet only while electricity flows through the wire.

Why must you stroke the nail in the same direction every time?

Stroking in one direction turns the nail into a magnet. Stroking backwards and forwards would undo the effect.
