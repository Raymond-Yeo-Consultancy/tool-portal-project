Key ideas

  • The respiratory system (nose, windpipe, lungs) takes air into the body and removes carbon dioxide.
  • In the lungs, oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide passes out of it.
  • The circulatory system (heart, blood vessels, blood) transports oxygen, digested food and water to all parts of the body, and carries carbon dioxide away.
  • The heart pumps the blood round the body.
  • The body needs oxygen and digested food to release energy. During exercise it needs more, so we breathe faster and the heart beats faster.
  • The respiratory, circulatory and digestive systems work together.

Think like a scientist

Why do you breathe faster when you run?

Your muscles work harder and need more energy. To release more energy they need more oxygen, and they produce more carbon dioxide. Faster breathing takes in more oxygen and removes more carbon dioxide.

Why does your heart beat faster too?

The heart must pump blood round the body more quickly, to deliver oxygen and digested food to the muscles faster and carry carbon dioxide away faster.

Where does the blood change from rich in carbon dioxide to rich in oxygen?

In the lungs. Carbon dioxide passes out of the blood into the air, and oxygen passes from the air into the blood.

How do the digestive, respiratory and circulatory systems work together?

The digestive system provides digested food. The respiratory system provides oxygen. The circulatory system carries both to every part of the body, where they are used to release energy.

How would you measure your own heart rate?

Feel your pulse at your wrist and count the beats in one minute. Do it sitting still, then again straight after exercise, and compare.
