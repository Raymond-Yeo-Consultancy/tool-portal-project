Plant Reproduction

How does a flower turn into a fruit? And how do seeds get to new places to grow?

Key ideas

  • The male parts of a flower are the anther and the filament. The female parts are the stigma, the style and the ovary, which contains ovules.
  • Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.
  • Fertilisation takes place when the male reproductive cell fuses with the egg cell in the ovule.
  • After fertilisation, the ovary develops into the fruit and the ovules develop into seeds.
  • Seeds are dispersed by wind, water, animals or splitting. This lets young plants grow away from the parent plant.

Think like a scientist

What is the difference between pollination and fertilisation?

Pollination is pollen landing on the stigma. Fertilisation comes later, when the male reproductive cell fuses with the egg cell inside the ovule. Pollination must happen first.

Why do many flowers have bright petals, scent and nectar?

They attract insects such as bees. As the insects feed, they carry pollen grains from one flower to another.

Why is it important for seeds to be dispersed far from the parent plant?

If all the seeds grew next to the parent, the plants would be overcrowded. They would compete for water, sunlight, mineral salts and space.

A fruit is light and has a wing-like structure. How is it likely to be dispersed? Why?

By wind. The wing helps it stay in the air longer, so the wind can carry it further away.
